## When the problem happens matters

A battery that goes dead after sitting all weekend calls for different checks than a window that stops working while you drive. Both are electrical problems. Start by describing what happened.

Tell Benchmark Automotive Service what stopped working and when you notice the problem. You do not need to know which part failed before making an appointment.

## Why does the battery keep going dead?

A dead battery does not, by itself, prove that the battery or alternator has failed. The battery, charging system and how you use the vehicle all matter. If the battery keeps going dead while parked, the technician may need to check for something drawing power while the vehicle is off.

Timing matters during that test. Modules can remain active for a period after shutdown. How much power the vehicle should draw, and how long its electronics take to shut down, depend on the vehicle, its equipment and the manufacturer's service information. There is no single current limit that should be applied to every car.

Subaru's [battery-drain diagnostic bulletin](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2025/MC-11024858-0001.pdf) explains why customer history and correct test conditions matter. Its specifications apply to the vehicles covered by that bulletin, rather than every vehicle visiting a repair shop.

## A working accessory can still have a circuit problem

A loose connection or excessive resistance can affect a circuit when it is operating. That can make a problem appear only under particular conditions. A visual check alone may not explain it.

Voltage-drop testing checks voltage lost along a circuit while current is flowing. It can help investigate connections and ground paths before replacing a part. Fluke explains this relationship in its [automotive electrical troubleshooting guidance](https://www.fluke.com/en-us/learn/blog/automotive/electrical-automotive-troubleshooting).

## Write down what you noticed

Useful details for an appointment include:

- The vehicle's year, make and model.
- Which accessory stopped working or the exact warning message displayed.
- How long the car had been parked when the battery was dead.
- Whether the problem happens every time or only occasionally.
- Any recent battery replacement, repair or accessory installation, such as a dash camera or remote starter.

A brief note made when the problem occurs can be more useful than trying to recall the details later. Report what you observed, even if the car is working again when you contact the shop.

## Check what the warning light means for your vehicle

A car can feel normal even when a warning light points to a safety problem. ABS, brake and airbag warnings mean different things.

Follow the instructions for the exact warning in your owner's manual. Ford's [warning-lamp guidance](https://www.fordservicecontent.com/Ford_Content/vdirsnet/OwnerManual/Home/Content?ProcUid=G1448512&Uid=G1448459&buildtype=webIt&countryCode=USA&div=f&languageCode=en&userMarket=USA&vFilteringEnabled=False&variantid=2458) illustrates those distinctions. It is a reference for the vehicles covered by that manual, not a diagnosis of yours.

## Not sure which service to request?

Electrical diagnostics covers battery drain, charging problems, wiring faults and accessories that work only some of the time. If your [car will not start](/services/car-wont-start), that page explains the no-start service. For rough running, hesitation or loss of engine power, see [engine and performance diagnostics](/services/engine-and-performance).

Symptoms can overlap. If you are unsure which appointment to request, [describe the problem to the shop](/contact).
