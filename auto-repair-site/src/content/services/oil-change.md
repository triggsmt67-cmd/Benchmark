## Oil and filter selected for your vehicle

An oil change starts with checking what your vehicle requires. At Benchmark Automotive Service, we choose the oil and filter using the specifications in your owner's manual.

That means checking more than whether the bottle says synthetic. The viscosity grade and required performance specifications matter too. API's [engine oil guide](https://www.api.org/products-and-services/engine-oil) explains oil quality marks and standards; your vehicle's requirements determine which product is appropriate.

Bring your vehicle details and any service records you have. If you don't know when the last oil change was done, tell us. We can discuss what's due without pretending an oil sample reveals the entire service history.

## Know the price before we start

We check the cost of the oil and filter before doing the work, especially on European vehicles. Some require products that cost more than a standard oil change.

An advertised special doesn't cover every oil, filter or vehicle requirement. If your vehicle needs something outside the offer, we'll explain the price before proceeding. Ask us to confirm what applies to your car when you contact us.

## What happens during the visit

We replace the engine oil and filter using the products required for your vehicle. The visit also gives us a chance to look for visible concerns and check accessible fluid levels as appropriate for the vehicle.

Before your car leaves, we double-check for oil leaks and other problems we can spot visually. That final check is part of our process, but it doesn't replace further testing when something needs investigation.

You receive a written list of findings, with priorities explained. We'll talk through what needs attention now, what you can plan and budget for, and what should be monitored and rechecked. Any additional work is discussed before it moves forward.

For more about that approach, see [general maintenance](/services/general-maintenance). Our [fluid services page](/services/fluid-services) explains how we use service history and condition when considering other fluid work.

## Allow time for your vehicle

An oil change usually takes 45–60 minutes at Benchmark. Certain European and high-end vehicles can need more time, so ask about timing when you arrange the visit.

If we find something that needs additional work, we'll explain the recommendation and discuss it with you. The usual oil-change time isn't a promise that every repair can be completed during that visit.

## When is your next oil change due?

Use the maintenance requirements for your vehicle, including time limits, mileage and any applicable oil-life monitor instructions. Towing or other operating conditions may affect the schedule, depending on the manufacturer's guidance.

There isn't one mileage interval or monitor percentage that fits every car. Oil color alone doesn't decide the timing, either.

If you're overdue, arrange service and tell us about low oil level, warning lights or new noises. We can't determine whether damage has occurred just from how far past the interval you've driven. Adding oil doesn't replace an oil and filter change or explain why the level dropped.

## Oil-pressure warning or maintenance reminder?

A low-oil-pressure warning is different from a reminder to change the oil. If a pressure warning appears while the engine is running, stop as soon as it's safe and switch the engine off. Follow your owner's manual and arrange assistance as needed.

Don't assume the warning means you can drive to an oil-change appointment. Ford's [oil-pressure warning guidance](https://www.fordservicecontent.com/Ford_Content/vdirsnet/OwnerManual/Home/Content?ProcUid=G2489838&Uid=G2489836&buildType=web&countryCode=USA&div=f&languageCode=EN&userMarket=BHS&vCode=&vFilteringEnabled=False&variantid=10878) illustrates the distinction; the instructions for your vehicle remain the reference.

If you aren't sure which message you're seeing, describe the exact wording when you contact us. You don't need to diagnose it yourself.
