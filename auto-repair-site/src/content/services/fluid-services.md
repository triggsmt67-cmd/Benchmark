## Which fluids are due on your vehicle?

If you don't know when the transmission or differential fluid was last changed, start there. Bring the service records you have, or tell us what you remember. We can work from that information instead of guessing at a maintenance history.

Benchmark Automotive Service handles coolant, brake, transmission, differential, transfer case and hydraulic power steering fluid service at our Missoula shop. The fluid, interval and service method need to fit the vehicle.

## How Benchmark decides what to recommend

We check fluid levels where the vehicle's design and service procedure allow. We also try to establish how many miles the fluid has been in use, then look at its actual condition.

Burnt fluid or visible metal debris gets our attention. Those findings may call for more investigation, rather than simply replacing the fluid and assuming the problem is solved.

We consider the manufacturer's maintenance requirements alongside those checks. A fluid sample doesn't replace a time or mileage requirement, and mileage alone doesn't explain a leak or a shifting problem. Tell us how you use the vehicle, including towing, so we can check the guidance that applies.

## The right fluid matters

Transmission fluid, coolant and gear oil aren't interchangeable products. Different vehicles can require different specifications, even when the fluids look similar.

Coolant color alone doesn't tell you whether two products are compatible. Gates discusses the risks of incompatible coolant mixtures in its [cooling-system service guidance](https://www.gates.com/content/dam/gates/home/knowledge-center/resource-library/tech-tips-bulletins/tt002-13.pdf). Use the specification required for the vehicle rather than choosing by color.

A coolant freeze-point check can help assess concentration. It doesn't, by itself, measure all the corrosion protection left in the fluid. Robinair describes what a [coolant refractometer measures](https://www.robinair.com/products/coolant-and-battery-refractometer).

Don't open a radiator or coolant reservoir cap while the system is hot. If you're unsure how to check a fluid safely, leave that check to the shop.

## Brake fluid service and bleeding after repairs

At Benchmark, when brake work opens the hydraulic system, such as replacing a master cylinder or caliper, we use our brake bleeding machine as part of the bleeding procedure to remove trapped air. The procedure needs to follow the requirements for that vehicle.

Bleeding after a repair and replacing old brake fluid address different concerns. Neither should be treated as a cure for every pedal problem. We need to understand what's happening before recommending the work.

If braking has changed or the pedal is sinking, stop driving as soon as you can safely pull over and arrange assistance. Follow your owner's manual for any brake warning. Don't assume topping off the reservoir or scheduling a flush makes the vehicle safe to drive.

For more about brake concerns, see [brake repair](/services/brake-repair).

## A symptom may need diagnosis first

A transmission that slips, an engine that runs hot or steering that has started making noise needs more than a routine maintenance recommendation. Low or degraded fluid may be part of the problem, but it isn't the only possibility.

Let us know about warning lights, leaks and changes in how the car drives when you contact us. That helps us plan an inspection rather than book a fluid change for a problem it may not fix.

For a temperature warning or steam, follow your vehicle's instructions and arrange assistance as needed. Our [engine overheating page](/services/engine-overheating) explains what to report before an inspection.

## What to bring to your appointment

Your vehicle's year, make, model and mileage give us a starting point. Service receipts help establish what's already been done. If you've added fluid yourself, let us know which product you used.

You don't need to inspect underneath the car or collect a sample before calling. Describe any fluid you've noticed on the ground and where it appeared, without assuming the puddle identifies the source.
